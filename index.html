Mode Bridge is the wallet-connected route for moving supported assets between Ethereum and Mode, an EVM-compatible OP Stack layer two. You either bridge into Mode to use apps there, or bridge back out to Ethereum — and the path you pick (canonical bridge versus a liquidity route) changes your wait time, your trust assumptions, and sometimes your received asset. This is an independent reference dashboard: it doesn't custody funds and it doesn't execute transactions, so confirm everything against the Mode documentation and your own wallet before signing.

What is Mode Bridge?

It's Mode's canonical bridge — a fork of the Optimism Standard Bridge — plus third-party routes shown alongside it in the Mode app. The canonical path connects only Ethereum Mainnet and Mode Mainnet, escrowing your asset on L1 and releasing the corresponding balance on L2 via OP Stack cross-domain messaging. Mode's docs list ETH, USDC, USDT and WBTC as canonical assets, and canonically bridged ETH arrives as native ETH on Mode (which is also the gas token), not a wrapped version. The app also surfaces routes powered by LI.FI and other liquidity bridges; those can be faster or reach more chains, but you take on that protocol's contracts and liquidity assumptions on top of Mode's. If you're parking size, the canonical route has the fewest moving parts — that's the general trade-off with any OP Stack bridge, not a Mode-specific quirk.

How bridging works

Connect an EVM wallet, pick source chain, destination, asset, amount, and a route — then approve (for ERC-20s) and sign the bridge transaction. Native ETH skips the approval step entirely; tokens need an allowance granted to the route's contract first, so you're signing two transactions, not one. On a canonical deposit, funds are locked in the L1 Standard Bridge and minted or released on Mode once the L2 picks up the message. A canonical withdrawal runs the other way: initiate on Mode, prove on Ethereum, sit through the challenge window, then finalize with a second Ethereum transaction. Your wallet controls every signature — the bridge never takes custody in the exchange sense, but escrowed L1 funds do sit in upgradeable contracts while their L2 counterparts exist.

Depositing to Mode

Depositing is the easy direction: send from Ethereum, and canonical deposits generally land on Mode within minutes. Two things to set up first. Add Mode Mainnet (chain ID 34443) to your wallet — testnet users want Mode Sepolia (919). And make sure you'll have ETH on the Mode side for gas; a first-time canonical ETH deposit covers you, but if you're bridging only USDC you'll arrive with funds you can't move until you get gas there. The address receiving on Mode is the same EVM address as your L1 wallet, so there's no separate account to create. Keep a little ETH on Ethereum too — you'll need it if you ever withdraw back.

Withdrawing back to Ethereum

Canonical withdrawals from Mode to Ethereum take about seven days because of the optimistic-rollup challenge period — plan around that, don't fight it. The flow is: initiate the withdrawal on Mode, submit a proof transaction on Ethereum once the state is published, wait out the challenge window, then send the finalization transaction on Ethereum to actually receive funds. Each Ethereum step costs L1 gas, so a small withdrawal can be uneconomic in fees even though the bridge itself charges nothing extra. If you need funds on L1 sooner, a liquidity bridge route can exit faster in exchange for its own fees and trust assumptions. Whatever you do, don't re-initiate the same withdrawal because the first one looks stuck — a pending canonical withdrawal is normal, not lost.

Time and fees

You pay source-chain gas plus whatever route costs the quote shows — the canonical bridge doesn't tack on a separate toll. In practice that means an Ethereum deposit costs L1 gas at whatever the network is charging that day, while actions starting on Mode cost pennies-level L2 gas priced in ETH. Third-party routes add their own provider or liquidity fees, which the quote screen should itemize; read it before confirming rather than after. Timing splits cleanly by direction: deposits to Mode are minutes, canonical withdrawals back carry the seven-day challenge period. If a quote looks surprisingly expensive, it's almost always L1 gas on the Ethereum leg — check gas prices and consider waiting for a quieter block rather than abandoning the route.

Is Mode Bridge safe?

Bridging removes exchange-custody risk but keeps smart-contract, upgrade, and phishing risk — no bridge is risk-free, audited or not. The honest risk list for this route: the bridge contracts are upgradeable, Mode currently relies on permissioned proposers and challengers, and any ERC-20 approval you grant is a standing allowance against a contract. L2BEAT's Mode profile is the place to check the current permissioning and upgrade setup for yourself. Phishing is the bigger day-to-day threat: fake bridge sites ranking for the exact name of the real one are a standard attack. Verify the URL, verify the contract address your wallet shows, and verify the allowance scope before signing — and treat unlimited approvals as something you revoke after large moves, not leave standing.

Problems and fixes

Most bridge problems are one of four things, and all four are fixable without panic. Wrong network: your wallet is on Ethereum but you're trying to act on Mode (or vice versa) — switch networks, don't re-send. No gas on the destination: funds arrived on Mode but you have no ETH there to move them; bridge a small amount of ETH over to unstick yourself. Stuck-looking withdrawal: check whether it's waiting on the proof step, the challenge window, or finalization — each stage needs its own transaction, and "pending" for days during the challenge period is expected behavior, not an error. Suspected fake site: if the URL or contract doesn't match what the official docs show, disconnect and walk away; you can verify Mode-side transactions on the Mode explorer. If a canonical transaction is confirmed on the source chain, the funds are in the bridge mechanism — what you're waiting on is messaging or finalization, not rescue.

Mode FAQ
Is this the official Mode Bridge site?

No. This is an independent reference dashboard — it doesn't custody assets or execute transactions. Always confirm the bridge interface, contract addresses, and route against the official Mode documentation before signing anything.

How long does Mode Bridge take?

Deposits from Ethereum to Mode generally complete within minutes. Canonical withdrawals back to Ethereum carry the optimistic-rollup challenge period of about seven days, plus a proof and a finalization transaction on Ethereum that you submit yourself.

How do I bridge to Mode?

Connect an EVM wallet, choose Ethereum as source and Mode (chain ID 34443) as destination, pick the asset and amount, then approve the token if it's an ERC-20 and sign the bridge transaction. Native ETH needs no approval. The receiving address on Mode is the same as your Ethereum address.

Do I need ETH on Mode before bridging?

Yes for transacting after you arrive — ETH is Mode's native gas token. A canonical ETH deposit gives you gas automatically, but if you bridge only USDC or another token, you'll hold funds you can't move until some ETH lands on your Mode address.

Is Mode Bridge safe to use?

It removes exchange-custody risk but retains smart-contract, upgradeability, and Mode protocol risks — the contracts are upgradeable and proposers and challengers are currently permissioned, as tracked on L2BEAT. Verify the site, the contract, and the allowance before every signature, and never treat any bridge as risk-free.

What's the difference between the canonical bridge and third-party routes?

The canonical bridge is Mode's fork of the OP Stack Standard Bridge: it connects only Ethereum and Mode, escrows assets on L1, and carries the seven-day withdrawal wait. Third-party liquidity routes shown in the app can be faster or reach more chains, but add that provider's contracts, fees, and trust assumptions on top.

Notes before you use Mode Bridge

Bridging to Mode is simple; bridging back is where people get surprised. Deposits land in minutes, so newcomers assume exits work the same way — they don't, the canonical route carries a seven-day challenge window and two Ethereum transactions you have to send yourself.

The common mistake is approving a token on a bridge site found through search ads without checking the URL or the contract the wallet is actually asking you to approve.

Before you sign:

  • Confirm the bridge site and the contract address in your wallet prompt against the official Mode docs — the L1 Standard Bridge proxy is 0x735aDBbE72226BD52e818E7181953f42E3b0FF21.
  • Check source and destination networks are what you intend (Mode Mainnet is chain ID 34443), and keep ETH for gas on BOTH sides.
  • Decide before depositing how you'll exit: canonical withdrawal (seven-day wait, L1 gas for proof and finalization) or a liquidity route with its own fees and assumptions.
